## 1. Name, description, slogan

- **Name:** Sweet Treats by Char
- **One line:** Custom cakes and cupcakes, made to order at home in Plaridel, Bulacan.
- **Slogan:** Baked with heart, made for your celebration.

## 2. Positioning and audience

A home bakery run by a self-taught baker. Families in and around Plaridel and Bulacan order personalised cakes for birthdays (mostly kids'), christenings, anniversaries and weddings, often with cupcakes and party giveaways. Reviews keep coming back to three points: the cakes look great, they're "not too sweet" and moist, and the prices are fair ("sulit"). Char is also easy to talk to. The site should feel like a small cake boutique, warm and personal, never like a factory.

## 3. Personality and voice

- **Personality:** warm, playful, celebratory, careful, honest, homey, proud of the craft.
- **Voice:** short and friendly sentences in plain English, with a little Taglish where it feels natural ("Order na!"). Talk about the occasion and the person being celebrated, not about "products". Don't use hype words, and don't make claims about awards, years in business or ingredients that the owner hasn't confirmed.

## 9. Design notes

- **Signature element:** a scalloped cake-board edge. The real cakes sit on scalloped paper boards, so section edges and photo frames use a fine scallop, in gold or blush, like a doily under a cake.
- Photos are the stars. Large, generous crops on calm blush backgrounds.
- The raspberry accent appears only on buttons and key highlights. Gold stays thin: hairlines and prices.
- Avoid: cartoon characters and brand logos, glitter and ribbon clip-art (the posters already do that; the site should feel calmer and more premium), stock bakery imagery, made-up reviews, and invented claims.
- Reviews stay in the customers' own Taglish. That's their charm.
